Making games for my mom

My mom got her first MacBook, and she was struggling.

Not because she isn't smart — she's one of the sharpest people I know — but because so much of a computer's "obvious" behavior isn't obvious at all. It's learned. The little Mac-specific mechanics that I do on autopilot — dragging a window, snapping it to one side, switching between two apps — were a wall for her. And every time she got stuck, she felt a little less confident, a little more like technology just wasn't for her.

That feeling is the real problem. Not the trackpad. Not the menu bar. The quiet story that starts to form: I'm bad at this.

So I decided to make her some games.

A window management "game"

The first one is about window management. Instead of a tutorial — which feels like homework — it's a tiny, low-stakes playground. Move this window here. Now make it fill the screen. Now put these two side by side. Little goals, instant feedback, no way to break anything, no one watching.

The point isn't to teach her a list of shortcuts. It's to let her practice the motions until they stop feeling scary — until the muscle memory shows up and the anxiety fades. Play does that in a way instruction never can. When something is a game, a mistake is just part of the game. You shrug and try again.

What I'm learning

A few things are becoming clear as I build these:

  • Confidence is the actual feature. The skill is a side effect. If she finishes feeling capable, the design worked.
  • Lower the stakes and people will explore. Fear of breaking something is the biggest blocker to learning technology. Take it away and curiosity comes back.
  • Designing for one person you love is clarifying. There's no persona, no committee. Just: does this help my mom? It's the most honest brief I've ever had.

I'm going to keep making these — one for window management, then probably one for files and folders, maybe one for keyboard shortcuts. I'll write about them here as I go.

Mostly, I just want her to feel like the computer is hers.
